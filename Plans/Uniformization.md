# Blueprint: uniformisation of once-punctured elliptic curves (U1)

Branch `wp-uniformization` of `lana-agents/oka`. Consumer: `lana-agents/orbicurve-cores`, branch
`wp-canlift27`, `Blueprint.md` §2.3a (piece **U1** of [CanLift] Prop. 2.7).

## 0. Target

The statement requested by the consumer (commit `8c21442` of `orbicurve-cores`), restated here
without depending on `orbicurve-cores` (`tr` is `Matrix.trace` of the underlying matrix):

```lean
theorem uniformization_oncePunctured (W : WeierstrassCurve ℂ) [W.IsElliptic] :
    ∃ (A B : SL(2, ℝ)) (π : ℂ → ℂ × ℂ),
      tr (A * B * A⁻¹ * B⁻¹) = -2 ∧ tr A ≠ 0 ∧
      (∀ z : ℍ, DifferentiableAt ℂ π z ∧ deriv π z ≠ 0) ∧
      (∀ z : ℍ, W.toAffine.Equation (π z).1 (π z).2) ∧
      (∀ x y : ℂ, W.toAffine.Equation x y → ∃ z : ℍ, π z = (x, y)) ∧
      (∀ z w : ℍ, π z = π w ↔ ∃ γ ∈ Subgroup.closure {A, B}, γ • z = w)
```

Mathematically: a holomorphic universal covering `ℍ → E(ℂ) ∖ {O}` whose deck group, lifted to
`SL(2, ℝ)`, is generated by two elements with commutator of trace `−2` (a once-punctured torus
group, signature `(1; ∞)`).

### (a) General Koebe uniformisation via Perron and Green's functions

Riemann surfaces as charted spaces; subharmonic functions on them; Perron families, Harnack,
barriers and the Dirichlet problem; Green's function on hyperbolic surfaces; universal covering
surface construction (path classes) with its Riemann surface structure; monodromy/harmonic
conjugates on simply connected surfaces; injectivity of `exp(−(g + i g*))`; the Riemann mapping
theorem. For `E ∖ O` there is an extra problem: `E ∖ O` itself is **parabolic** (no Green's
function: bounded subharmonic functions extend over the puncture), so Green's function must be
built on the universal cover directly, and proving that it exists there needs a separate
hyperbolicity certificate (a nonconstant bounded holomorphic function on the cover), which is
essentially the modular-function input of route (b′) anyway. Estimate **25–35k lines**; gives the
general theorem.

### (b) Riemann mapping for the simply connected cover

"Universal cover of `ℂ/Λ ∖ 0` via `ℂ ∖ Λ`, then the Riemann mapping theorem on it." The crux is
that the universal cover is an abstract simply connected Riemann surface, not a plane domain, and
the Riemann mapping theorem needs an **injective** holomorphic map of it into `ℂ` with non-dense
image to start from. Constructing that injective map *is* the uniformisation problem (planarity of
simply connected surfaces is Koebe's theorem). Liouville/Picard-type arguments only show that the
cover is not `ℂ` or `ℙ¹` once uniformisation is known. The extremal problem (maximise `|f′(p)|`
over holomorphic `f : S → 𝔻`, `f(p) = 0`) has an extremal by Montel, and Koebe's square-root trick
proves it is **surjective**, but not injective without an injective competitor. So (b) as stated
does not avoid (a).

### (b′) Chosen: Koebe–Fisher–Hubbard–Wittner iteration for the plane domain `ℂ ∖ Λ`, run downstairs

[FHW] Y. Fisher, J. H. Hubbard, B. S. Wittner, *A proof of the uniformization theorem for arbitrary
plane domains*, Proc. AMS 104 (1988) 413–418: if a plane domain `U` has a covering by a subdomain
`U₀ ⊆ 𝔻`, then iterating Koebe's square-root maps produces the universal covering `𝔻 → U`. Two
observations make this cheap here.

1. **The starting covering comes from `j`, which `heights` already has.** `j : ℍ → ℂ` has fibres
   the `SL(2, ℤ)`-orbits, and `SL(2, ℤ)` acts properly discontinuously and (by an explicit check on
   the fundamental domain) freely modulo `±1` off the orbits of `i` and `ρ`. So `j` is a covering
   `ℍ ∖ j⁻¹(B) → ℂ ∖ B` with `B = {j(i), j(ρ)}`. An affine map `T` sends two lattice points onto
   `B`, so `T(ℂ ∖ Λ) ⊆ ℂ ∖ B`, and `U₀ := Cayley(j⁻¹(T(ℂ ∖ Λ)))` is `𝔻` minus a closed discrete set,
   covering `ℂ ∖ Λ`. ([FHW] use the modular `λ` for `ℂ ∖ {0, 1}`; `j` with its two branch values
   serves equally well, because only *some* covering by a bounded domain is needed.)
2. **No abstract universal cover is needed.** [FHW] take a limit of maps `Ũ → Uₙ` on the abstract
   universal cover. We take instead the limit of the *downstairs* maps
   `ϖₙ = q₁ ∘ ⋯ ∘ qₙ : Uₙ → U₀` (plane domains throughout) and prove directly that the limit
   `ψ : 𝔻 → U₀ → ℂ ∖ Λ` is a covering map, by producing local sections: for a disc `Δ ⊆ ℂ ∖ Λ` and
   `z ∈ ψ⁻¹(Δ)` the lifts `σₙ : Δ → Uₙ` of `Δ` through the coverings (lifting criterion, `Δ` convex)
   form a normal family whose limit `σ` is a holomorphic section of `ψ` through `z`. Sections through
   distinct points have disjoint images, so `Δ` is evenly covered. That the limit does not escape
   `U₀` is Hurwitz's theorem, applicable because `𝔻 ∖ U₀` is discrete.

The Koebe step: given `D(0, r) ⊆ U ⊆ 𝔻` with `a ∉ U`, `|a| = r`, choose `b² = −a` and put
`q(z) = z (z − c)/(1 − c̄ z)`, `c = −2b/(1 + |b|²)`. Then `q = m_a ∘ sq ∘ m_b` (`m_t(z) = (z+t)/(1+t̄z)`),
so `q` is a 2-sheeted covering `𝔻 ∖ {−b} → 𝔻 ∖ {a}` and `U′ := 𝔻 ∩ q⁻¹(U)` covers `U`. The
factorisation gives `|q(z)| ≤ s(s + γ)/(1 + γ s)` for `|z| = s`, `γ = |c| = 2√r/(1 + r) < 1`, hence
`D(0, h(r)) ⊆ U′` with the explicit continuous `h(r) = (−γ(1−r) + √(γ²(1−r)² + 4r))/2 > r`. So the
in-radii increase to `1`.

After `ψ : ℍ → ℂ ∖ Λ` (compose with the Cayley map) everything is elementary:
deck transformations of `ℍ → ℂ ∖ Λ → E ∖ O` are biholomorphisms of `ℍ`, hence in `PSL(2, ℝ)`
(Schwarz lemma); the group is generated by the two edge-pairings `A`, `B` of a lifted fundamental
parallelogram (open–closed argument on the tiling); `A⁻¹B⁻¹AB` is the monodromy of a small loop
around a lattice point, not trivial and not hyperbolic (removable singularity + open mapping),
not elliptic (free action), so parabolic, and its trace is `−2` rather than `+2` because trace `2`
would force a common fixed point of `A`, `B` on `∂ℍ`, and then conjugating the commutator by powers
of `A` or `B` produces deck transformations accumulating at a point.

Estimate **7–11k lines**, of which the uniformisation of `ℂ ∖ Λ` is about half. Gives a genuine
universal covering by the disc for every plane domain that has *some* covering by a subdomain of
`𝔻` with discrete complement (in particular all `ℂ ∖ (discrete set with ≥ 2 points)`), not the
general uniformisation theorem.

### (c) Schwarzian / Fuchsian ODE

Uniformise `ℙ¹ ∖ {e₁, e₂, e₃, ∞}` (the quotient `(E ∖ O)/±1` as an orbifold) by ratios of solutions of
`y″ + ¼(Σ …)y = 0` with the correct accessory parameter. Existence of the accessory parameter is
itself proved by uniformisation or by a continuity method; needs linear ODEs with regular
singularities, monodromy, Schwarzian derivatives. Not cheaper; **20k+ lines** with an unsolved
existence step.

### (d) Continuity method on Fricke space

For every Fricke triple `(x, y, z)`, `x² + y² + z² = xyz`, `x, y, z > 2`, the explicit
group `Γ` is discrete (done in `orbicurve-cores`, ping-pong) and `Γ\ℍ ∪ {cusp}` is a compact genus-1
surface; one must show `Γ\ℍ ∪ {cusp} ≅ ℂ/Λ` (a nonzero weight-2 cusp form for arbitrary `Γ`: Poincaré
series do not converge in weight 2), that the resulting map Fricke space → `ℍ/SL(2, ℤ)` is
continuous, open (Teichmüller-type injectivity) and proper, hence onto. **20–30k lines**, most of it
Riemann-surface theory that (b′) avoids.

**Decision:** (b′).
